Frequently Asked Questions

What is cervical cancer? ▼

Cervical cancer develops in the cervix, the lower part of the uterus. It is primarily caused by infection with the Human Papillomavirus (HPV). When detected early, cervical cancer is highly treatable and preventable.

How can I prevent cervical cancer? ▼

Prevention involves: HPV vaccination (ideally before sexual activity), regular Pap smear or HPV screening tests, maintaining a healthy lifestyle, avoiding smoking, practicing safe sex, and maintaining overall health through proper nutrition and exercise.

What is HPV and how does it cause cervical cancer? ▼

HPV (Human Papillomavirus) is a common virus spread through sexual contact. While most HPV infections clear on their own, persistent infection with high-risk types (like HPV 16 and 18) can lead to cellular changes that may develop into cervical cancer if left undetected.

Is the HPV vaccine effective? ▼

Yes, HPV vaccines are over 99% effective at preventing infection with HPV types 16 and 18 when administered before exposure. The vaccine is recommended for ages 11-26, though it can be given up to age 45. It works best when given before sexual activity begins.

What is a Pap smear test? ▼

A Pap smear is a screening procedure where cells from the cervix are collected and examined under a microscope. It can detect precancerous or cancerous changes early, allowing for treatment before cancer develops. It's one of the most effective cancer screening tests available.

How often should I get screened? ▼

Screening guidelines vary, but generally: Pap smear every 3-5 years for women 21-65 years old, or HPV testing every 5 years. It's best to discuss your individual screening schedule with your healthcare provider based on your risk factors.

What are the early symptoms of cervical cancer? ▼

Early cervical cancer often has no symptoms. However, as it progresses, symptoms may include abnormal vaginal bleeding, pelvic pain, pain during intercourse, and unusual vaginal discharge. Any persistent symptoms should be discussed with a healthcare provider.

What treatment options are available? ▼

Treatment depends on the cancer stage and may include surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, or a combination of these. Early-stage cancers are generally more treatable. Your healthcare team will recommend the best approach for your specific situation.

Where can I find support? ▼

Support is available through healthcare providers, cancer support organizations, support groups for survivors, and online communities. Our resources page provides links to various support organizations and counseling services.
